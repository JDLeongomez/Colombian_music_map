Colombian music

Colombia's music divides broadly into six regions, each shaped by the country's geography and history. This page collects examples from each, plotted roughly where they originated. Select a point on the map to hear it and read more.

A note on this selection

Colombia is sometimes called the Land of a Thousand Rhythms: over 1,025 folkloric rhythms have been documented, shaped by a geography of mountains, rainforest, plains and coasts that kept regions musically distinct from one another, and by different a mix of Indigenous, African and European influences (I lean towards recognising the depth of Afro-Colombian influence in particular). This page only scratches the surface of that.

I have a background in music, but I'm not a musicologist or an expert in Colombian traditional music; treat this as a personal, incomplete set of examples, not a survey. I tend to prefer videos of live performances, and I lean towards older and more traditional music over very modern styles: you won't find Shakira, Karol G or reggaetón here, even though reggaetón dominates popular Colombian music today. The goal isn't to be comprehensive, just to point at some of the variety.

Each example is also tagged traditional, fusion or non-traditional (shown on the map as a circle, triangle or square):

Traditional
Genre and performance style stay close to the folk or ceremonial roots, with little to no modern fusion.
Fusion
A traditional genre or instrumentation deliberately blended with modern genres or production — jazz, pop, electronic, and so on.
Non-traditional
A contemporary genre — rock, hip hop, electronic, synthpop — with little direct grounding in a traditional genre, even where the theme or culture is unmistakably Colombian.

If you think something's wrong, missing, or could be better, the source is on GitHub: corrections and additions are welcome: github.com/JDLeongomez/Colombian_music_interactive_map.
